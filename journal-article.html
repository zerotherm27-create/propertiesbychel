The Quiet Economics of a Tree-Lined Street

Makati · A field note from the villages

Walk any village street in Makati at seven in the morning and you will notice what the listing photographs never show: the ceiling. Fifty-year acacias close ranks overhead, the light arrives pre-filtered, and the temperature drops a degree or two the moment you pass the gate. None of this appears on a title. All of it appears in the price.

Buyers new to the villages sometimes read the premium as nostalgia. It is closer to arithmetic. A mature canopy means the street was planned once, properly, and then left alone, which usually means the lots are regular, the setbacks honoured, and the neighbours long-tenured. Trees this old are evidence of governance.

A fifty-year tree is the cheapest due diligence you will ever commission. It testifies that nothing reckless has happened on that street for fifty years.

The arithmetic runs further. Streets that keep their trees keep their households; villages that keep their households almost never flood the market. Supply in the established villages is not merely low; it is structurally reluctant. Homes pass to heirs, heirs hold, and the rare open sale draws a quiet queue that formed years before the sign would have gone up, if a sign were ever permitted.

What this means for a buyer

First, patience is not optional; it is the entry fee. The right house on the right street may take seasons to appear, and the buyer who is ready (financing arranged, counsel briefed, intentions clear) is the one the street chooses. Second, price the street before the house. Structures can be renovated; a canopy, an orientation, and a set of neighbours cannot.

And for a seller

If you hold one of these houses, understand what you are actually selling. The improvements may be dated; the street is not. Presentation should lead with the setting (the approach, the light, the quiet) and let the buyer's architect solve the rest. Sellers who market the structure alone routinely underprice the address.

The villages will never be a fast market, and that is precisely their function in a portfolio. They are where Philippine capital goes to slow down.
