District Guide · 01 · Metro Manila

Makati

The established core, where Philippine prime property learned its manners.

The Atmosphere

Old trees,
quiet money.

Makati holds two markets in one postcode. Inside the gates, Forbes, Dasmariñas, Urdaneta, San Lorenzo, Bel-Air, Magallanes, streets run under fifty-year canopies and houses trade between families, often without a listing. Outside them, Salcedo and Legazpi Villages carry the metro's most mature condominium stock, walkable to weekend markets, galleries, and the business district's best tables.

The rhythm is settled rather than showy. Mornings belong to the parks and the school runs; evenings to a restaurant scene that never needed to announce itself. For principals who value predictability, of title, of neighbours, of resale, Makati remains the reference market against which everything else is priced.

What Trades Here

The stock

Legacy villagesGated single-family homes on 800–2,500 sqm lots; village association consent governs; deepest discretion, thinnest supply
Prime condominiumsSalcedo & Legazpi mature towers plus a short list of new builds; strongest leasing depth in the metro
Trophy penthousesA handful change hands each year, mostly quietly; provenance and building services decide value

Market Notes

The reading

Village supply stays structurally scarce, lots are finite and heirs increasingly hold. Condominium value divides sharply by building management quality; two towers a street apart can price a third differently.

Buyers should budget patience for village association processes. Sellers holding well-kept older units near the parks are in a stronger position than headline averages suggest.
